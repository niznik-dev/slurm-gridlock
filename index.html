How the merge order is decided

      
The scheduler lets traffic merge one level at a time. Departments take turns first, then the groups inside each department, then the people inside each group. Your group's place comes before your own, so a group that has been driving a lot lately holds every member back.

      
Each reading shows how much of its share that group or person has been driving lately. Groups under their share cruise along smoothly. The further over they go, the more their car sputters and smokes, like it’s overdue for the shop. Groups with no recent driving wait in the car park, first to merge.

    Slurm Queue Traffic Report reads your file inside this browser tab. There is no server, no tracking, and nothing is saved after you close or refresh the page.
    
  
    It’s a simplified picture built from what Slurm shows every user, so treat the numbers as close estimates, not an audit. Groups with their own private queues may be missing or undercounted.
    
  
    It explains priority, not start time. A job at the front of the line can still wait for enough free hardware, and the scheduler can let small jobs slip through gaps around big ones.
